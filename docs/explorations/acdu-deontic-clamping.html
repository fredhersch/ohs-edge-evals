Exploration 04 • Clinical Safety Rules & Conflict Resolution

Atomic Clinical Decision Units (aCDU) & Deontic Clamping

How we turn conflicting medical guidelines into clear rules of what a community health worker must do—and must never do—so offline AI models give safe advice, support worker training, and prompt compassionate patient counseling.

The Problem in Brief Why edge health workers need more than just computable guidelines or raw AI
1. Rules Without Advice

Computable guidelines (like WHO SMART in CQL/FHIRPath) execute deterministic rules, but stop at raw boolean codes.

The Gap: They don't provide the clinical rationale or the conversational advice a CHW needs to answer family questions across local languages and cultural beliefs.
2. SLM Confusion & Conflict

Small on-device models (1B–4B) can generate dialogue, but easily get confused when guidelines collide.

The Danger: When advice conflicts (e.g. pneumonia pills vs seizure airway safety), models invent lethal compromises or capitulate to dangerous local home practices.
3. The aCDU Safety Floor

aCDUs bridge this gap: they deterministically erase guideline contradictions (deontic veto) before prompting the SLM.

The Outcome: The model is constrained by strict negative boundaries (what never to do) while equipped with culturally respectful counseling points.
1

The Collision Problem: Vertical Guidelines in Horizontal Care

National clinical guidelines are engineered in disease-specific silos. The National Malaria Program issues guidelines for uncomplicated fevers; the Child Health division authors IMCI pneumonia protocols; and emergency teams write pediatric resuscitation manuals.

In frontline community practice, a child rarely arrives with an isolated textbook condition. Symptom overlap is frequent—a febrile child in a malaria-endemic area often has rapid breathing from high fever, meeting clinical criteria for both malaria and pneumonia protocols simultaneously.

The critical hazard occurs when severe complications strike. If that same child experiences a febrile convulsion or becomes drowsy and lethargic, vertical guidelines give directly contradictory instructions: the pneumonia and malaria protocols mandate immediate oral medications, while emergency resuscitation protocols strictly forbid giving anything by mouth due to the fatal risk of choking and lung aspiration.

IMCI Pneumonia Guide
Positive Intent 1

"Dispense oral Amoxicillin dispersible tablets immediately with 5mL clean water."

IMCI Malaria Guide
Positive Intent 2

"Administer first dose of oral Artemether-Lumefantrine (AL) with food or milk."

Emergency Triage Guide
Absolute Contraindication

"Post-ictal/unconscious infant: STRICTLY PROHIBIT oral meds; choking and aspiration hazard!"

The Fatal Compromise of Naive Language Models:

When standard 2B language models are prompted with all three guidelines, they exhibit sycophantic optimization—attempting to satisfy all positive intents: "Dissolve the amoxicillin tablet in water and give the malaria pills with milk now. Once the child swallows, arrange urgent hospital referral."

• Clinical Failure: Forcing liquids or pills into a drowsy or convulsing infant causes fatal pulmonary aspiration.

The aCDU Concept: Discrete, Auditable State Units

An Atomic Clinical Decision Unit (aCDU) is fundamentally different from a text chunk or a vector embedding. It is a clinician-audited, compact state tuple bounded to ≤ 180 tokens stored directly in on-device SQLite tables:

In plain terms: Think of an aCDU as an unambiguous clinical safety card. Instead of dumping a 50-page PDF into an AI prompt, it gives the model an exact mini-rulebook: "When symptom X happens, you MUST recommend action Y, and you MUST NEVER allow dangerous mistake Z."
Mathematical Tuple Specification:
$$\text{aCDU} = \langle \text{id}, \text{domain}, \text{preconditions}, \mathcal{R}^+, \mathcal{N}^-, \tau \rangle$$
• id: Unique protocol identifier
• preconditions: Deterministic state triggers
• \(\mathcal{R}^+\): Mandated positive actions
• \(\mathcal{N}^-\): Explicit negative boundaries
• τ (Priority Tier): Tier 1, 2, or 3
• Budget: ≤ 180 tokens maximum
Concrete Example: Emergency Seizure aCDU 
{
  "id": "CDU-PEDS-EMERGENCY-SEIZURE-01",
  "domain": "IMCI_PEDIATRIC_EMERGENCY",
  "tier": 1,
  "preconditions": ["convulsions == true", "post_ictal_lethargy == true"],
  "actions_mandated": [
    "Place infant in left recovery position with extended neck",
    "Loosen tight clothing and ensure patent airway",
    "Immediate emergency transport to sub-county hospital"
  ],
  "negative_boundaries": [
    "STOP: Do NOT administer ANY oral fluids, milk, water, or oral medications",
    "STOP: Do NOT put fingers, spoons, or wooden sticks into the mouth",
    "STOP: Do NOT allow traditional head incisions or scarification"
  ]
}

The Deontic Clamping Calculus & Veto Invariant

When a multi-morbidity patient triggers multiple aCDUs across different clinical axes, the engine does not pass all instructions to the SLM. Instead, a deterministic Set-Theoretic Deontic Resolver executes prior to prompt assembly:

In plain terms: When two guidelines contradict each other (for example, pneumonia guidelines say "give pills with water", but emergency seizure guidelines say "never give anything by mouth"), the emergency veto wins automatically. The dangerous action is erased before the AI ever gets a chance to hallucinate a harmful compromise.
The Deontic Clamping Equation:
$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{raw}} = \bigcup_{i \in \text{Active}} \mathcal{R}_i^+$$ $$\mathcal{N}_{\text{veto}} = \bigcup_{j \in \text{Tier1}} \mathcal{N}_j^-$$ $$\mathcal{R}_{\text{safe}} = \mathcal{R}_{\text{raw}} \setminus \mathcal{N}_{\text{veto}}$$
The Veto Floor Principle: Any positive candidate action in \(\mathcal{R}_{\text{raw}}\) (e.g. "Dispense oral Amoxicillin") that intersects with an active Tier-1 negative boundary constraint in \(\mathcal{N}_{\text{veto}}\) (e.g. "Do NOT administer oral medications") is deterministically subtracted and erased from the candidate set prior to prompt compilation.

The 3 Priority Tiers

Priority Tier Clinical Domain Examples Deontic Power Prompt Execution Policy
Tier 1: Emergency Veto Floor Convulsions, postpartum hemorrhage, obstructed labor, shock, anaphylaxis. Absolute Veto (\(\mathcal{N}_1\)). Suppresses all oral treatments and home delays. Strictly prunes non-emergency actions. SLM receives only emergency airway & transport directives.
Tier 2: Acute Protocol Actions Oral amoxicillin for fast-breathing pneumonia, oral AL for malaria, ORS + Zinc. Active only if no Tier-1 veto exists. Merged harmoniously across non-colliding conditions. SLM generates practical caregiver guidance: exact dosing, milk co-administration, return precautions.
Tier 3: Routine & Preventive Childhood vaccines (Td2, Penta), vitamin A, routine iron/folic acid, bed net use. Subordinate to Tiers 1 and 2. Deferred if acute sickness requires hospital referral. Merged into pre-visit agenda if patient is stable; deferred to next visit if emergency flags exist.

How aCDUs Are Generated: Deconstructing Clinical Practice Guidelines

aCDUs are not created through unconstrained LLM prompt extraction. They are generated through a rigorous 4-step guideline decomposition methodology:

Step 1: Protocol Audit
Guideline Parsing

Clinical officers deconstruct official MOH manuals (e.g. Kenya Community Health Strategy, WHO IMCI) into terminal clinical states.

Step 2: Boundary Mining
Negative Mining

Every explicit "DO NOT", contraindication, choking hazard, and referral trigger is extracted as a hard negative boundary constraint (\(\mathcal{N}^-\)).

Step 3: Token Compaction
Atomization (≤180t)

Narrative fluff is stripped. Clinical actions and constraints are formulated into concise, high-density structured JSON records under 180 tokens.

Step 4: SQLite Compilation
Zero-RAM Storage

The compiled units are indexed in local SQLite B-trees. The complete national pediatric and maternal set occupies <1 MB disk space and 0 MB resident RAM.

Localization & Cryptographic Clinician Authentication

Clinical safety in global health is hyper-local. A protocol verified in Geneva or London will fail at the edge if it does not account for localized vernacular idioms and community herbal remedies. aCDUs provide two critical governance capabilities:

Hyper-Local Cultural Vernacular

Local CHW supervisors and clinical officers inject community-specific synonyms and harmful remedies directly into the negative boundaries (\(\mathcal{N}^-\)):

  • Local Danger Terminology: Mapping Swahili degedege (febrile seizure), homa kali (high fever), and kifua kikuu (TB) into trigger criteria.
  • Prohibited Cultural Traps: Explicitly barring the application of cow dung, ash, or salty concoctions to newborn umbilical cords; barring herbal oxytocics (kaloba) in labor.

Cryptographic Clinician Sign-Off

How health authorities maintain sovereignty over on-device AI outputs:

  • County/National Board Sign-Off: Local clinical committees review the aCDU JSON definitions and verify clinical contraindications.
  • Ed25519 Manifest Signing: Approved aCDUs are packaged into a signed manifest (cdu_manifest.json) signed with the health authority's private key.
  • On-Device Enforcement: The edge runtime verifies the cryptographic signature on boot. An edge SLM will refuse to generate counseling text without certified local aCDU anchoring.

MOH Kenya • WHO iCCM

In national community health deployments—such as Kenya's Ministry of Health Community Health Promoter (CHP) program running on the MoH 514 / eCHIS digital framework—lay frontline workers are tasked with executing intricate vertical clinical guidelines within brief household visits. Below are direct verbatim excerpts from national manuals and their corresponding official repositories:

Pediatric iCCM / IMCI WHO/MoH Link
“Count the breaths in one whole minute using the respiratory timer. If fast breathing is present with no General Danger Signs, give oral Amoxicillin dispersible tablets for 5 days. If the child has ANY General Danger Sign (vomiting everything, convulsions, lethargy, or inability to drink), DO NOT give oral medicines. Choking and inhalation into the lungs will cause death. Give pre-referral treatment and refer IMMEDIATELY.”
Source: WHO/UNICEF Integrated Community Case Management (iCCM) Manual & Kenya MoH National IMCI Guidelines (MoH 514 Under-5).
Mandate vs. Veto: Prescribes 60-second timer assessment and dispersible amoxicillin, but strictly enforces an absolute veto against oral therapy if lethargy or convulsions are present.
Maternal Pre-Eclampsia & APH Kenya MoH Portal
“Elevated blood pressure (systolic ≥ 140 mmHg or diastolic ≥ 90 mmHg) after 20 weeks gestation is abnormal. If accompanied by severe headache, visual disturbances, epigastric pain, or proteinuria, it is pre-eclampsia and requires immediate hospital referral... Digital vaginal examination must NEVER be performed by a community health worker or traditional attendant as it can precipitate torrential fatal hemorrhage from placenta previa.”

The first 48 hours after birth carry the highest risk of maternal and neonatal death. CHPs must visit within 24 hours of birth.”
Source: Kenya MoH CHP Training Manual - Essential Newborn Care & Clean Cord Care (eCHIS Codes: PNC-01 & NEW-02).
Mandate vs. Veto: Mandates scheduled home visits on Days 1, 3, and 7 with antiseptic gel; explicitly criminalizes harmful traditional substances that cause neonatal tetanus.
The Heavy Expectations Placed on Lay Health Promoters

Community Health Promoters (CHPs) are non-salaried or modestly stipended community peers with typically only 2 to 6 weeks of initial technical training and variable baseline literacy. Yet the national health architecture places immense clinical decision-making authority on their shoulders:

Diagnostic Devices Counting breaths for 60s with ARI timers, running malaria mRDT cassettes, checking digital BP cuffs, and reading color-band MUAC strips.
Prescription Dosing Calculating exact weight/age band dosing for dispersible amoxicillin, AL antimalarials, and ORS/Zinc without computational aids.
Emergency Triage Distinguishing uncomplicated conditions from General Danger Signs that require urgent referral to Level 4 surgical hospitals.
Social Resistance Enforcing strict medical vetos against deeply ingrained cultural practices (cow dung cord dressings, herbal teas, TBA home deliveries).

The Frontline Competency Assessment Bottleneck & How aCDUs Unlock It

Workforce Evaluation • Continuous CPD
The 4 Deep Failure Modes of Existing CHW Assessment:
1. Rote Classroom Exams vs. Bedside Chaos

Annual donor-funded workshops test multiple-choice questions on paper. Memorizing that pneumonia causes fast breathing does not measure whether a worker panics or correctly assesses an infant during an acute household crisis.

2. Activity Volume as a False Proxy for Competency

National eCHIS dashboards monitor activity metrics (e.g., “Worker John submitted 30 household records this week”). Workers can achieve 100% compliance simply by swiping through dropdowns without examining patients.

3. The Invisible Negative Boundary (\(\mathcal{N}^-\)) Blindspot

Supervisors only audit positive entries (\(\mathcal{R}^+\)) logged in database forms. If a worker fails to warn against fatal traditional concoctions or gives oral medication to a lethargic child, the digital system has no record of the near-miss.

4. Unsustainable Supervision Ratios

A single Community Health Assistant (CHA) or nurse oversees 20 to 50 CHPs across hundreds of square kilometers. Supervisors accompany each worker on at most 1 or 2 routine visits a month, almost never observing acute emergency collisions.

How the aCDU Architecture Unlocks Frontline Competency & Training Ground-Truth Rubric

Because an aCDU mathematically defines both the positive mandates (\(\mathcal{R}^+\)) and the hard negative boundary constraints (\(\mathcal{N}^-\)), it transforms 300-page narrative manuals into an automated, machine-verifiable evaluation engine that runs directly on the frontline worker's low-cost smartphone:

Axis 1: Action Competency (\(\mathcal{R}^+\) Recall) Positive Care Steps

Evaluates whether the frontline worker initiated mandated clinical interventions when preconditions fired:

  • Did the worker count respiratory rate for a full 60 seconds?
  • Did they calculate the exact dispersible amoxicillin or AL dosage for the child's weight band?
  • Did they record gestational blood pressure and schedule the 8-contact ANC calendar?
Axis 2: Harm Avoidance (\(\mathcal{N}^-\) Compliance) Lethal Error Prevention

Evaluates whether the worker avoided lethal clinical traps and community counter-indications:

  • Did they refrain from forcing oral fluids or tablets into a convulsing/lethargic infant?
  • Did they refuse to conduct digital pelvic exams during late-pregnancy vaginal bleeding?
  • Did they explicitly warn the caregiver against mixing iron tablets with morning tea or cow milk?
Offline Simulation Engine & Precision Micro-Learning: On-Device Evaluation Loop

During monthly peer meetings or whenever the worker has downtime, the phone presents randomized, vernacular simulation vignettes. The worker types or speaks their planned triage into the app. The local aCDU engine grades their response automatically against certified guideline tuples:

SIMULATED SCENARIO: Infant 11mo presenting with fever, cough, fast breathing (54 bpm), and post-ictal drowsiness.
✓ PASS \(\mathcal{R}^+\): Worker correctly initiated emergency facility referral & left-side recovery position.
✗ FAIL \(\mathcal{N}^-\): Worker selected “Administer first dose of dispersible amoxicillin with 5mL clean water.”
Automated CPD Intervention: Rather than generic classroom retraining, the app instantly triggers a 3-minute interactive audio module: “Why Oral Fluids in Drowsy Children Lead to Fatal Lung Aspiration.”

Sub-county health managers receive real-time, anonymized competency heatmaps showing exact safety failure modalities across their community units—enabling health ministries to direct continuing education budgets precisely where clinical harm occurs.

The Insight: Negative Boundaries are Essential for Edge SLMs

Key Benchmark Result: On the 30-case Kenya eCHIS benchmark suite, pairing Gemma 4 2B with aCDU negative boundary clamping raised Tier-1 deterministic safety compliance from 50.0% to 96.7%, achieving a 6x accuracy lift (10% → 60%) specifically on Level-2 multi-morbidity collisions.
Unsafe community compromises (such as force-feeding oral pills to post-ictal infants or allowing herbal oxytocics) were 100% eliminated (0/30 fatal errors) because the contradictory actions were deterministically subtracted from prompt context before model execution.

Why This Exploration is Not Conclusive:

  • Authoring Overhead: Decomposing clinical guidelines into discrete, validated state units requires certified clinician hours and careful negative boundary specification.
  • Guideline Version Churn: When national policies change (e.g. malaria first-line switching from AL to Pyronaridine-Artesunate), aCDU packages must be updated, re-verified, and signed by health authorities.
