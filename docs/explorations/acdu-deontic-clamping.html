Exploration 04 • Clinical Safety Floors & Conflict Resolution

Atomic Clinical Decision Units (aCDU) & Deontic Clamping

Why small 2B language models cave into adversarial prompts and dangerous cultural traps, and how micro-protocol negative boundaries (`STOP: Do NOT...`) raise clinical safety compliance from 50% to 96.7%.

1

The Problem: Sycophancy & Hallucinated Compromise on 2B Models

Small language models are vulnerable to conversational pressure. In low-resource frontline settings, health workers routinely encounter complex community dilemmas:

  • Dangerous Traditional Practices: Family demands putting cow dung or herbal paste on an infected umbilical cord.
  • Out-of-Scope Requests: Mother requests injectable antibiotics or IV fluids at home for an infant in respiratory distress.
  • Colliding Morbidities: Patient has severe malaria with convulsions AND acute cough; baseline SLMs attempt oral amoxicillin while ignoring immediate hospital transfer.
The Baseline Failure Mode: An unconstrained 2B model seeks to be helpful. In our adversarial tests, Gemma 2 2B produced lethal compromises like: "You may allow the traditional herbal tea in moderation while waiting for the health center" or "Community health workers should administer oral cotrimoxazole immediately", directly violating national MOH scopes of practice.
2

The Exploration: aCDU Architecture & Pre-Prompt Deontic Pruning

We developed the Atomic Clinical Decision Unit (aCDU) framework. Rather than injecting broad textbook guidelines, clinical protocols are atomized into strictly formatted units under 180 tokens:

1. Trigger Condition

Evaluated deterministically via FHIRPath over the patient bundle (e.g. Obs(BP) > 140/90 AND Gestation > 20w).

2. Mandated Action

Concrete protocol step the CHW must execute (e.g. Immediate emergency referral to Level 4 Hospital).

3. Negative Boundary

Explicit deontic prohibitions (e.g. STOP: Do NOT give oral sedatives; do NOT advise home bedrest).

3

The Insight: Negative Boundaries are Essential for Small Models

Key Finding: On the 30-case Kenya eCHIS benchmark, adding aCDU negative boundary clamping raised Tier-1 deterministic safety compliance from 50.0% to 93.3% (strict) / 96.7% (soft) on Gemma 4 2B. Unsafe community compromises were completely eliminated.

Why This Exploration is Not Conclusive:

  • Conversational Tone Stiffness: When heavily clamped with `STOP: Do NOT`, 2B models can adopt a scolding, punitive tone that risks alienating patients in real-world counseling.
  • Authoring & Curation Burden: Writing and certifying thousands of negative boundary pairs across all primary care conditions requires clinical consensus and ongoing maintenance.
