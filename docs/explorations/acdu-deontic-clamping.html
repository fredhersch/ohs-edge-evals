Atomic Clinical Decision Units (aCDU) & Deontic Clamping

The anatomy of fused state micro-units, set-theoretic collision resolution, national eCHIS alignment, frontline worker competency evaluation, and empathetic patient engagement prompts for offline edge language models.

1

The Collision Problem: Vertical Guidelines in Horizontal Care

National clinical guidelines are engineered in disease-specific silos. The National Malaria Program issues guidelines for uncomplicated fevers; the Child Health division authors IMCI pneumonia protocols; and emergency teams write pediatric resuscitation manuals.

At the household level, however, a child rarely presents with a pure textbook single condition. A 14-month-old infant routinely presents with fast breathing (Pneumonia), positive mRDT (Malaria), AND generalized convulsions (Danger Sign). When multiple guidelines fire simultaneously:

IMCI Pneumonia Guide
Positive Intent 1

"Dispense oral Amoxicillin dispersible tablets immediately with 5mL clean water."

IMCI Malaria Guide
Positive Intent 2

"Administer first dose of oral Artemether-Lumefantrine (AL) with food or milk."

Emergency Triage Guide
Absolute Contraindication

"Post-ictal/unconscious infant: STRICTLY PROHIBIT oral meds; choking and aspiration hazard!"

The Fatal Compromise of Naive Language Models:

When standard 2B language models are prompted with all three guidelines, they exhibit sycophantic optimization—attempting to satisfy all positive intents: "Dissolve the amoxicillin tablet in water and give the malaria pills with milk now. Once the child swallows, arrange urgent hospital referral."

• Clinical Failure: Forcing liquids or pills into a drowsy or convulsing infant causes fatal pulmonary aspiration.

The aCDU Concept: Discrete, Auditable State Units

An Atomic Clinical Decision Unit (aCDU) is fundamentally different from a text chunk or a vector embedding. It is a clinician-audited, compact state tuple bounded to $\le 180$ tokens stored directly in on-device SQLite tables:

Mathematical Tuple Specification:
$$ ext{aCDU} = \langle ext{id}, ext{domain}, ext{preconditions}, \mathcal{R}^+, \mathcal{N}^-, au angle$$
• id: Unique protocol identifier
• preconditions: Deterministic state triggers
• R+: Mandated positive actions
• N-: Explicit negative boundaries
• τ: Priority Tier (1, 2, or 3)
• Budget: ≤ 180 tokens maximum
Concrete Example: Emergency Seizure aCDU 
{
  "id": "CDU-PEDS-EMERGENCY-SEIZURE-01",
  "domain": "IMCI_PEDIATRIC_EMERGENCY",
  "tier": 1,
  "preconditions": ["convulsions == true", "post_ictal_lethargy == true"],
  "actions_mandated": [
    "Place infant in left recovery position with extended neck",
    "Loosen tight clothing and ensure patent airway",
    "Immediate emergency transport to sub-county hospital"
  ],
  "negative_boundaries": [
    "STOP: Do NOT administer ANY oral fluids, milk, water, or oral medications",
    "STOP: Do NOT put fingers, spoons, or wooden sticks into the mouth",
    "STOP: Do NOT allow traditional head incisions or scarification"
  ]
}

The Deontic Clamping Calculus & Veto Invariant

When a multi-morbidity patient triggers multiple aCDUs across different clinical axes, the engine does not pass all instructions to the SLM. Instead, a deterministic Set-Theoretic Deontic Resolver executes prior to prompt assembly:

The Deontic Clamping Equation:
$$\mathcal{R}_{ ext{raw}} = igcup_{i \in ext{Active}} \mathcal{R}_i^+$$ $$\mathcal{N}_{ ext{veto}} = igcup_{j \in ext{Tier1}} \mathcal{N}_j^-$$ $$\mathcal{R}_{ ext{safe}} = \mathcal{R}_{ ext{raw}} \setminus \mathcal{N}_{ ext{veto}}$$
The Veto Floor Principle: Any positive candidate action in $\mathcal{R}_{ ext{raw}}$ (e.g. "Dispense oral Amoxicillin") that intersects with an active Tier-1 negative boundary constraint in $\mathcal{N}_{ ext{veto}}$ (e.g. "Do NOT administer oral medications") is deterministically subtracted and erased from the candidate set prior to prompt compilation.

The 3 Priority Tiers

Priority Tier Clinical Domain Examples Deontic Power Prompt Execution Policy
Tier 1: Emergency Veto Floor Convulsions, postpartum hemorrhage, obstructed labor, shock, anaphylaxis. Absolute Veto ($\mathcal{N}_1$). Suppresses all oral treatments and home delays. Strictly prunes non-emergency actions. SLM receives only emergency airway & transport directives.
Tier 2: Acute Protocol Actions Oral amoxicillin for fast-breathing pneumonia, oral AL for malaria, ORS + Zinc. Active only if no Tier-1 veto exists. Merged harmoniously across non-colliding conditions. SLM generates practical caregiver guidance: exact dosing, milk co-administration, return precautions.
Tier 3: Routine & Preventive Childhood vaccines (Td2, Penta), vitamin A, routine iron/folic acid, bed net use. Subordinate to Tiers 1 and 2. Deferred if acute sickness requires hospital referral. Merged into pre-visit agenda if patient is stable; deferred to next visit if emergency flags exist.

How aCDUs Are Generated: Deconstructing Clinical Practice Guidelines

aCDUs are not created through unconstrained LLM prompt extraction. They are generated through a rigorous 4-step guideline decomposition methodology:

Step 1: Protocol Audit
Guideline Parsing

Clinical officers deconstruct official MOH manuals (e.g. Kenya Community Health Strategy, WHO IMCI) into terminal clinical states.

Step 2: Boundary Mining
Negative Mining

Every explicit "DO NOT", contraindication, choking hazard, and referral trigger is extracted as a hard negative boundary constraint ($\mathcal{N}^-$).

Step 3: Token Compaction
Atomization (≤180t)

Narrative fluff is stripped. Clinical actions and constraints are formulated into concise, high-density structured JSON records under 180 tokens.

Step 4: SQLite Compilation
Zero-RAM Storage

The compiled units are indexed in local SQLite B-trees. The complete national pediatric and maternal set occupies <1 MB disk space and 0 MB resident RAM.

Localization & Cryptographic Clinician Authentication

Clinical safety in global health is hyper-local. A protocol verified in Geneva or London will fail at the edge if it does not account for localized vernacular idioms and community herbal remedies. aCDUs provide two critical governance capabilities:

Hyper-Local Cultural Vernacular

Local CHW supervisors and clinical officers inject community-specific synonyms and harmful remedies directly into the negative boundaries ($\mathcal{N}^-$):

  • Local Danger Terminology: Mapping Swahili degedege (febrile seizure), homa kali (high fever), and kifua kikuu (TB) into trigger criteria.
  • Prohibited Cultural Traps: Explicitly barring the application of cow dung, ash, or salty concoctions to newborn umbilical cords; barring herbal oxytocics (kaloba) in labor.

Cryptographic Clinician Sign-Off

How health authorities maintain sovereignty over on-device AI outputs:

  • County/National Board Sign-Off: Local clinical committees review the aCDU JSON definitions and verify clinical contraindications.
  • Ed25519 Manifest Signing: Approved aCDUs are packaged into a signed manifest (cdu_manifest.json) signed with the health authority's private key.
  • On-Device Enforcement: The edge runtime verifies the cryptographic signature on boot. An edge SLM will refuse to generate counseling text without certified local aCDU anchoring.

Frontline Reality: Kenya eCHIS Guidelines, Worker Evaluation & Patient Engagement

Connecting abstract state calculus to national Ministry of Health protocols, frontline competency audits, and human-centered clinical counseling.

National eCHIS Protocols & Frontline Worker Expectations

In national community health deployments—such as Kenya's Ministry of Health Community Health Promoter (CHP) program running on the MoH 514 / eCHIS digital framework—lay frontline workers are tasked with executing intricate vertical clinical guidelines within brief household visits:

Pediatric iCCM / IMCI eCHIS Under-5
Clinical Expectation: Count respiratory rate for a full 60 seconds with an ARI timer. For cough + fast breathing (>50 bpm in 2–11m, >40 bpm in 12–59m), classify as non-severe pneumonia, dispense oral dispersible Amoxicillin (250mg bid × 5 days), and observe the first dose.
eCHIS Hard Veto: If any General Danger Sign (GDS) appears—convulsions, lethargy, or vomiting everything—oral medication is strictly contraindicated. Immediately administer pre-referral rectal artesunate (if malaria suspected) and facilitate emergency transport.
Maternal Pre-Eclampsia MoH 514 ANC-04
Clinical Expectation: Measure serial blood pressure on all pregnant women after 20 weeks. If SBP ≥ 140 or DBP ≥ 90 mmHg, screen for danger signs: frontal headache, epigastric pain, visual flashing, and facial edema.
eCHIS Hard Veto: Never dispense oral antihypertensives (nifedipine/labetalol) or home sedatives; never attempt parenteral magnesium sulfate at home. Absolute prohibition against digital vaginal examinations in late pregnancy bleeding (ANC-05).
Newborn Cord Care MoH 514 PNC-01
Clinical Expectation: Conduct home visits on Day 1, 3, and 7 postpartum. Inspect umbilical stump for redness, swelling, or foul discharge. Ensure daily application of 7.1% chlorhexidine digluconate gel or dry cord care until separation.
eCHIS Hard Veto: Strictly prohibit the application of cow dung, ash, salty water, kerosene, or binding coins to the umbilical stump, which are pervasive root causes of neonatal tetanus and cord sepsis.
The Frontline Worker Dilemma: Checklist Fatigue vs. Cognitive Overload

Frontline health workers are typically trusted community peers with 2 to 6 weeks of initial technical training. Standard digital eCHIS tools subject them to rigid, 40-step linear questionnaires. When a multi-morbid case presents (e.g. a child with cough, fever, and post-ictal lethargy), standard apps fire disjointed alerts from separate program modules. Without explicit deontic clamping, workers are left to reconcile contradictory vertical guidelines in their heads—either defaulting to checklist bypass or giving dangerous contradictory advice.

Dual Utility 1: Objective Frontline Worker Evaluation & Continuous Training

Workforce Development

Evaluating community health workers has historically been fraught with subjectivity—relying on periodic paper logbook audits or superficial checklist completions. Because an aCDU mathematically defines both the positive mandates ($\mathcal{R}^+$) and the negative boundary constraints ($\mathcal{N}^-$), it provides a rigorous, dual-axis evaluation rubric:

Axis 1: Positive Mandate Recall ($\mathcal{R}^+$ Adherence) Action Competency

Measures whether the frontline worker correctly initiated the required clinical interventions when preconditions fired:

  • Did the worker count respiratory rate for 60 seconds?
  • Did they calculate the exact dispersible amoxicillin or AL dosage for the child's weight band?
  • Did they record gestational blood pressure and schedule the 8-contact ANC calendar?
Axis 2: Negative Boundary Compliance ($\mathcal{N}^-$ Safety) Harm Avoidance

Measures whether the worker successfully avoided lethal clinical traps and community counter-indications:

  • Did they refrain from forcing oral fluids or tablets into a convulsing infant?
  • Did they refuse to conduct digital pelvic exams in third-trimester bleeding?
  • Did they explicitly warn the caregiver against mixing iron tablets with morning tea or cow milk?
Offline Simulation Engine & Precision Micro-Learning:

During monthly peer review or facility supervision meetings, the on-device engine presents randomized, local clinical simulation vignettes. The worker types or speaks their planned actions into the app. The aCDU parser automatically grades their plan against the mathematical tuple:

SIMULATION: Infant 11mo presenting with fever, cough, fast breathing (54 bpm), and post-ictal drowsiness.
✓ PASS R+: Correctly triggered immediate emergency facility referral & left-side recovery position.
✗ FAIL N-: Worker selected "Administer first dose of dispersible amoxicillin with 5mL water".
Targeted CPD Triggered: Serving 3-minute interactive audio module: "Why Oral Fluids in Drowsy Children Lead to Fatal Lung Aspiration".

Sub-county supervisors receive granular safety heatmaps, allowing continuing education funds to be spent on exact failure modalities rather than repetitive generic classroom refresher courses.

Dual Utility 2: Generative Prompts for Frontline Patient Engagement & Empathy

Frontline Copilot

The single most common complaint from rural caregivers is that digital health tools convert empathetic community health workers into detached data-entry clerks who stare at smartphone screens. Because the aCDU deterministically locks down clinical safety ($\mathcal{R}_{\text{safe}} = \mathcal{R}_{\text{raw}} \setminus \mathcal{N}_{\text{veto}}$), the on-device SLM can be safely unfettered to generate warm, conversational, vernacular counseling scripts tailored to the caregiver's household reality:

Pediatric Pneumonia Counseling Fast Breathing
STANDARD eCHIS OUTPUT: Fast breathing detected (54 bpm). Dispense Amoxicillin 250mg bid x 5d. Review in 48h.
aCDU ENGAGEMENT PROMPT: "Mama Juma, look closely at his little chest with me. See how his ribs pull inward every time he breathes? That tells us his lungs are working hard to fight an infection. This white tablet will melt in a clean spoon of water just like breastmilk. Let us dissolve the first one together now so you can watch him take it."
Caregiver Impact: Visual demonstration overcomes maternal pill aversion; caregiver observes safe tablet dispersion firsthand.
Maternal Pre-Eclampsia Triage BP 148/94
STANDARD eCHIS OUTPUT: Severe gestational hypertension. Alert: Emergency Referral to Level 4 Hospital required.
aCDU ENGAGEMENT PROMPT: "Halima, your blood pressure is high today and that throbbing headache is a warning sign from your body. We must go to the sub-county hospital right away. Let me help you call your husband, and while we organize the motorbike, lie down comfortably on your left side to keep blood flowing strongly to the baby."
Caregiver Impact: Replaces cold bureaucratic alarms with de-escalating, actionable support; overcomes transport hesitation and spousal delay.
Newborn Cord De-escalation Harm Reduction
STANDARD eCHIS OUTPUT: Apply Chlorhexidine 7.1%. Do not apply cow dung, ash, or traditional salves.
aCDU ENGAGEMENT PROMPT: "Grandmother may kindly suggest putting ash or oil on the belly button to help the stump dry faster. Please explain to her that dust carries invisible germs that give babies lockjaw. Keeping it clean with this blue liquid lets the cord heal safely without pain or fever."
Caregiver Impact: Respects the grandmother's loving intention while framing the scientific boundary without shaming the family.
The Frontline Takeaway: When clinical constraints and safety vetos are enforced deterministically by aCDUs, edge language models are liberated to do what they do best: bridge the human communication gap, translate rigid clinical protocols into empathetic vernacular dialog, and empower frontline community health workers to be healers rather than data clerks.

The Insight: Negative Boundaries are Essential for Edge SLMs

Key Benchmark Result: On the 30-case Kenya eCHIS benchmark suite, pairing Gemma 4 2B with aCDU negative boundary clamping raised Tier-1 deterministic safety compliance from 50.0% to 96.7%, achieving a 6x accuracy lift (10% → 60%) specifically on Level-2 multi-morbidity collisions.
Unsafe community compromises (such as force-feeding oral pills to post-ictal infants or allowing herbal oxytocics) were 100% eliminated (0/30 fatal errors) because the contradictory actions were deterministically subtracted from prompt context before model execution.

Why This Exploration is Not Conclusive:

  • Authoring Overhead: Decomposing clinical guidelines into discrete, validated state units requires certified clinician hours and careful negative boundary specification.
  • Guideline Version Churn: When national policies change (e.g. malaria first-line switching from AL to Pyronaridine-Artesunate), aCDU packages must be updated, re-verified, and signed by health authorities.
