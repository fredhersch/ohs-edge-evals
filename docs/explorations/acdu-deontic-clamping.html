Exploration 04 • Clinical Safety Floors & Conflict Resolution

Atomic Clinical Decision Units (aCDU) & Deontic Clamping

Why small 2B language models cave into adversarial prompts and dangerous cultural traps, and how micro-protocol negative boundaries (`STOP: Do NOT...`) raise clinical safety compliance from 50% to 96.7%.

1

The Problem: Sycophancy & Hallucinated Compromise on 2B Models

Small language models are vulnerable to conversational pressure. In low-resource frontline settings, health workers routinely encounter complex community dilemmas:

  • Dangerous Traditional Practices: Family demands putting cow dung or herbal paste on an infected umbilical cord.
  • Out-of-Scope Requests: Mother requests injectable antibiotics or IV fluids at home for an infant in respiratory distress.
  • Colliding Morbidities: Patient has severe malaria with convulsions AND acute cough; baseline SLMs attempt oral amoxicillin while ignoring immediate hospital transfer.
The Baseline Failure Mode: An unconstrained 2B model seeks to be helpful. In our adversarial tests, Gemma 2 2B produced lethal compromises like: "You may allow the traditional herbal tea in moderation while waiting for the health center" or "Community health workers should administer oral cotrimoxazole immediately", directly violating national MOH scopes of practice.
2

The Exploration: OHS Workflow `$apply`, PlanDefinitions & aCDU Deontic Clamping

In the Open Health Stack (OHS) architecture, deterministic clinical reasoning is executed via an $apply-style operation on HL7 FHIR PlanDefinition resources. The engine evaluates clinical logic written in FHIRPath or CQL against the patient bundle, outputting structured recommendation codes.

However, raw PlanDefinition actions only declare what to do (positive clinical intent); they do not encode explicit prohibitions against dangerous community practices, nor do they resolve multi-morbidity collisions during natural language generation. We map each recommendation code to an Atomic Clinical Decision Unit (aCDU) bounded to $\le 180$ tokens:

1. PlanDefinition Trigger ($apply)

Evaluated deterministically via FHIRPath/CQL in the OHS Workflow engine (e.g. mRDT == 'positive' and gestation > 20w). Emits recommendation codes.

2. Mandated Action ($\mathcal{R}$)

Concrete guideline protocol step the health worker must execute (e.g. Urgent referral to CEmONC facility).

3. Negative Boundary ($\mathcal{N}$)

Explicit deontic prohibitions (e.g. STOP: Do NOT give oral medications; do NOT allow traditional oxytocic herbs).

How This Operates Across 3 Graduated Complexity Scenarios
Case 1: Simple (Single Rule Fires)

Child with positive malaria RDT and no danger signs. PlanDefinition $apply emits IMCI-ACT-MAL-AL → maps to CDU-PEDS-MAL-AL. SLM translates into a single 3-bullet script with weight-adjusted dosing, co-administration with fatty food/milk, and return precautions.

Case 2: Intermediate (Multiple Non-Conflicting Rules Across Modules)

ANC mother at 28w with mild anemia (Hb 10.4) and due for routine Td2 booster. Two separate PlanDefinitions fire: ANC-ACT-IRON-FOLIC and ANC-ACT-IMMZ-TD2. Deontic check confirms $\mathcal{N}_{\text{anemia}} \cap \mathcal{R}_{\text{immz}} = \emptyset$. The SLM harmonizes a single counseling visit covering both injection prep and iron side-effects without alarm.

Case 3: High Complexity (Multiple Rules Fire + Deontic Safety Override)

Infant with pneumonia fast breathing, malaria fever, AND active convulsions (degedege). PlanDefinitions fire oral Amoxicillin, oral AL, and emergency referral. The Tier-1 Emergency aCDU (CDU-EMERGENCY-SEIZURE-01) asserts: STOP: Do NOT give ANY oral fluids, food, or medications (aspiration/choking risk). The deontic conflict resolver strips oral amoxicillin and oral AL from the prompt context ($R_{\text{safe}} = R_{\text{raw}} \setminus N_{\text{Tier1}}$). The SLM is clamped to generate an emergency transport script only.

The Frontline Dilemma: From Multi-Output Chaos to Clamped Clarity
❌ FROM THIS: Raw Multi-Output Flood

The CHW sees 3 alerts: "Give Amox DT", "Give oral AL", and "Refer immediately". If an un-gated SLM attempts to summarize them, it hallucinatingly compromises: "Give the amoxicillin and AL pills with water, then transfer to hospital." Result: Fatal aspiration choking.

✅ TO THIS: aCDU Deontic Clamping

Box 1 Tier-1 negative boundary vetoes all oral actions before prompt synthesis. The 2B SLM receives only emergency instructions: 1. Left recovery position, 2. Strictly FORBID oral pills/fluids, and 3. Rapid hospital escort. Zero cognitive confusion for the worker.

Pre-Prompt Deontic Conflict Resolver Logic Deterministic Veto Floor 
# Deterministic conflict resolution before SLM prompt synthesis:
# R_safe = R_raw \ N_bundle (Negative prohibitions override general guidance)
for tier1_unit in active_acdus_by_tier(tier=1):
    for negative_rule in tier1_unit.negative_boundaries:
        # Veto and purge contradictory lower-tier positive actions
        retrieved_actions = [a for a in retrieved_actions if not violates(a, negative_rule)]

# Box 2 Generative SLM receives the safe, non-conflicting subset
injected_prompt = compile_prompt(patient_summary, retrieved_actions, tier1_unit.negative_boundaries)

The Insight: Negative Boundaries are Essential for Small Models

Key Finding: On the 30-case Kenya eCHIS benchmark, adding aCDU negative boundary clamping raised Tier-1 deterministic safety compliance from 50.0% to 93.3% (strict) / 96.7% (soft) on Gemma 4 2B. Unsafe community compromises were completely eliminated.

Why This Exploration is Not Conclusive:

  • Conversational Tone Stiffness: When heavily clamped with `STOP: Do NOT`, 2B models can adopt a scolding, punitive tone that risks alienating patients in real-world counseling.
  • Authoring & Curation Burden: Writing and certifying thousands of negative boundary pairs across all primary care conditions requires clinical consensus and ongoing maintenance.
