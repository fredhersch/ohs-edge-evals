Atomic Clinical Decision Units (aCDU) & Deontic Clamping

How we resolve contradictory clinical guidelines deterministically before prompting edge models—enforcing strict negative boundaries while empowering offline models to counsel families with empathy and cultural nuance.

The Problem in Brief Why edge health workers need more than just computable guidelines or raw AI
1. Rules Without Advice

Computable guidelines (like WHO SMART in CQL/FHIRPath) execute deterministic rules, but stop at raw boolean codes.

The Gap: They output raw diagnostic codes without practical guidance or conversational rationale.
2. Contextualized Advice

Health workers must adapt recommendations to real home contexts: water access, distance, literacy, and beliefs.

The Need: Empathetic, culturally sensitive counseling in vernacular languages (Swahili, Dholuo, Hausa).
3. Collision & Compromise

Small on-device models (1B–4B) generate dialogue, but hallucinate lethal compromises when guidelines collide.

The Danger: Trying to satisfy oral pill rules while airway safety rules forbid oral intake.
4. The aCDU Safety Floor

aCDUs bridge this: deterministically enforcing negative boundaries before the SLM generates dialogue.

The Result: 100% negative boundary recall and zero aspirational compromises.

The Collision Problem: Vertical Programs in Frontline Practice

Why vertical clinical guidelines produce lethal contradictions when combined on-device

National guidelines are engineered in silos: Malaria programs mandate oral antimalarials, Pneumonia protocols mandate immediate amoxicillin, and Emergency Triage strictly prohibits giving anything by mouth during seizures. When a febrile child has a seizure, these guidelines collide simultaneously.

IMCI Pneumonia Guide
Positive Mandate 1

"Dispense oral Amoxicillin tablets immediately with clean water."

IMCI Malaria Guide
Positive Mandate 2

"Administer first dose of oral Artemether-Lumefantrine (AL) with food/milk."

Emergency Resuscitation
Absolute Contraindication

"Post-ictal/unconscious infant: STRICTLY PROHIBIT oral intake; choking hazard!"

The Danger: Naive Language Models Attempt Lethal Compromises View Evals Benchmark

When standard 2B models are prompted with all three guidelines, they try to please everyone: "Dissolve the amoxicillin tablet in water and give the malaria pills with milk now. Once the child swallows, arrange urgent referral." Forcing liquids into a drowsy or seizing child causes fatal pulmonary aspiration.

Identified in benchmark cases #07 (Seizure + Fast Breathing) & #14 (Hypoglycemia + Oral Antimalarial) in the 30-Case Kenya eCHIS Report ↗.

The Solution: Two-Box Architecture & Deontic Veto Invariant

Pre-filtering safety bounds deterministically before the generative model produces output

Box 1: Deterministic aCDU Floor SQLite FTS5

Clinician-curated micro-rules bounded to ≤ 180 tokens stored in local SQLite tables. When guidelines conflict, a mathematical Deontic Veto Invariant executes:

Veto: ∀ r ∈ R+, if Collides(r, N-) ⇒ VETO(r)

Any Tier-1 emergency negative boundary (e.g. "Never give oral meds to convulsing child") immediately strips and overrides lower-priority positive recommendations.

Box 2: Constrained Edge SLM Gemma 4 / MedGemma

The on-device model receives only the conflict-free aCDU packet. It focuses its parameters entirely on what language models do best:

  • Translating clinical directions into vernacular dialects (Swahili, Dholuo)
  • Addressing parental distress with empathetic, clear communication
  • Gently redirecting dangerous local home practices (e.g. cord ash)

Anatomy of an aCDU State Record

JSON-LD / SQLite

Instead of storing raw guideline prose in vectors, each unit is a discrete tuple with explicit positive mandates (R+) and fatal negative boundaries (N-): 

{
  "id": "CDU-PEDS-EMERGENCY-SEIZURE-01",
  "domain": "IMCI_PEDIATRIC_EMERGENCY",
  "priority_tier": 1,
  "preconditions": "patient.age < 5 AND patient.convulsing == true",
  "positive_mandates": [
    "Turn child to left lateral recovery position immediately",
    "Clear airway; do not insert fingers or sticks into mouth",
    "Urgent hospital referral with pre-referral rectal diazepam if trained"
  ],
  "negative_boundaries": [
    "NEVER administer oral medications, fluids, or foods during or after convulsion",
    "NEVER restrain limbs during active seizure",
    "NEVER place cow dung, ash, or paraffin in nostrils or mouth"

  "vernacular_counseling": {
    "swahili": "Usimpe dawa wala maji kwa mdomo mtoto akiwa na degedege; mlaze kwa upande wa kushoto.",
    "cultural_reframing": "Address evil spirit beliefs respectfully while ensuring physical airway safety."
  }
}
